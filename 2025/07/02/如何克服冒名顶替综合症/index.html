如何克服冒名顶替综合症

发表于2025-07-02|更新于2025-07-02|网络文章
|浏览量:

A Phd should be a really positive experience. So as Phd students, you get to take a really deep dive into a subject that you’re interested in. You get to develop a rare and valuable skills and expertise, and you get to work with and learn from really, really smart people. It can also open the door to a career in research where you get to pursue your own ideas and use your creativity to ultimately contribute something new to the world. And of course, you get to call yourself doctor at the end, which is pretty cool. I believe that it should be an amazing experience and one that you look back on years from now with fondness.
博士应该是一个非常积极的经历。作为博士生，你可以深入研究你感兴趣的主题。你可以发展出罕见而宝贵的技能和专业知识，你可以与真正聪明的人一起工作并从他们身上学习。它还可以打开研究事业的大门，在那里你可以追求自己的想法，并利用你的创造力最终为世界贡献新的东西。当然，最后你可以称自己为医生，这很酷。我认为这应该是一次了不起的经历，多年后你会带着喜爱回望。

But this isn’t everybody’s experience, and a lot of students end up burned out and miserable. Now, there are all kinds of reasons why this might happen, but today I’d like to focus on one particularly common problem that can negatively affect every aspect of your experience. Not only, your ability to do your work, but also ultimately other aspects of your life as well, and that is imposter syndrome.
但这并不是每个人的经历，很多学生最终都筋疲力尽，痛苦不堪。现在，有各种各样的原因导致这种情况的发生，但今天我想专注于一个特别常见的问题，它会对你体验的各个方面产生负面影响。不仅仅是你的工作能力，还有你生活的其他方面，这就是冒名顶替综合症。

So an imposter is basically someone who’s pretending to be something that they’re not.
所以冒名顶替者基本上是那些假装成自己不是的东西的人。

And imposter syndrome is the feeling that you’re not good enough or that you don’t belong there. And it usually also comes with comparison with other people, usually negative. So thinking that you’re maybe not as good as the people around you, but not always, I can come with the worry about what other people will think about you, which is often actually not really about what other people think, but maybe what you think about yourself. And it also comes with the fear of being found out. So if you don’t believe you’re good enough, what if somebody finds out that you somehow kind of snuck your way in at the same time, you might also have a certain image of how good you should be or a certain expectation of yourself. So we have this combination of low confidence, but with very high self expectations.
“冒名顶替综合症” 是指你感觉不够好或者不属于那里。这通常还伴随着与其他人的比较，通常是负面的。因此，我认为自己可能没有周围的人那么好，但并非总是如此，我可能会担心其他人会怎么看你，这通常不是关于其他人的想法，而是你对自己的看法。它还伴随着害怕被发现。所以，如果你不相信自己足够优秀，如果有人发现你同时以某种方式偷偷溜进来，你可能也对自己应该有一定的印象或对自己有一定的期望。所以我们有这种低自信，但自我期望非常高的组合。

Now, these beliefs, they might not be conscious, but they can kind of sit in the dark shadows of your mind, quietly influencing everything that you do. And it can influence your behavior and your decisions and your confidence in many different ways. But the general pattern is this. So if you see yourself as an impostor, as someone who doesn’t deserve to be there, the natural tendency will be to hide.
现在，这些信念可能不是有意识的，但它们可以坐在你心灵的阴影中，静静地影响你所做的一切。它可以以许多不同的方式影响你的行为、决定和信心。但一般的模式是这样的。因此，如果你认为自己是一个冒名顶替者，作为一个不应该出现在那里的人，那么自然的倾向就是躲藏起来。

So when something goes wrong or when you don’t know how to do something, then you don’t seek help. You don’t want to ask a stupid question. So generally, you’ll avoid talking to people and you’ll stay small and kind of try to be invisible, igno also mean that you don’t fully invest yourself or you undermine yourself in different ways.
因此，当出现问题或当你不知道如何做某事时，你就不会寻求帮助。你不想问一个愚蠢的问题。通常情况下，你会避免与人交谈，你会保持小规模，并试图隐形，这也意味着你不会充分投资自己，或者你会以不同的方式削弱自己。

Because subconsciously, if you don’t put your full efforts in, then no one can really judge your full potential. And that might manifest itself through procrastination or perfectionism. And if things are going wrong, you might also take it as further evidence that you’re not good enough. So it just reinforces that impostor feeling.
因为潜意识里，如果你不全力以赴，那么没有人能真正判断你的全部潜力。这可能通过拖延或完美主义表现出来。如果事情出了问题，你也可能把它作为你不够好的进一步证据。所以它只会强化那种冒名顶替者的感觉。

But then this doesn’t just happen when things are going wrong. Even when things go right, if you feel like you’re an imposter, you might think, well, I was lucky, or I don’t deserve any praise, or it might mean that you don’t want to show your work to other people in case it gets rejected. If you’re going for publications or maybe it gets criticized or if somebody discovered a problem. So imposter syndrome, if it’s unchecked, becomes this kind of negative cycle where the more you work, the less confident you get and the harder it gets to do the work, which just reinforces the self doubt. So the question is, how do we reverse this cycle? So how can you approach the work in such a way that you get more confident over time and so that you can face the problems that arise with energy and enthusiasm and take pride in your achievements?
但这不仅仅是在事情出了问题时发生的。即使事情顺利，如果你觉得自己是个冒名顶替者，你可能会想，好吧，我很幸运，或者我不值得任何赞扬，或者这可能意味着你不想把你的工作展示给其他人，以防它被拒绝。如果你要去出版物，或者它可能会受到批评，或者有人发现了问题。所以冒名顶替综合症，如果不加控制，就会变成这种负面循环，你工作得越多，你就越不自信，工作就越难，这只会加强自我怀疑。所以问题是，我们如何逆转这个循环？那么，你怎样才能以一种方式处理工作，使你随着时间的推移变得更加自信，从而能够充满活力和热情地面对出现的问题，并为自己的成就感到自豪？

So there are two basic ways I would like to approach this. First of all, what I’d like to do is try and undo some of the false beliefs or assumptions that lead to imposter syndrome. And then I’d like to talk about how we can build confidence and skills so that you can become the best researcher that you can be and hopefully also enjoy the process.
所以我想用两种基本方法来处理这个问题。首先，我想做的是尝试消除导致冒名顶替综合症的一些错误信念或假设。然后我想谈谈我们如何建立信心和技能，使你成为最好的研究员，并希望也能享受这个过程。

So where does imposter syndrome come from? In my own case, it was there from the very first day of my Phd. So on that first day, way back in September 2003, so almost 21 years ago, which is a little bit scary, there was this induction event where all kinds of different people from the department came and spoke to all the new Phd students. And a lot of it was about administration and those kind of boring things. But there’s one talk in particular that I still remember 20 two-one years later.
那么冒名顶替综合症从何而来？在我自己的情况下，从我博士的第一天开始，它就在那里。所以在大约21年前的第一天，也就是2003年的九月，有一个有点可怕的入职活动，来自系里的各种各样的人前来与所有新的博士生交谈。而且很多都是关于管理和那些无聊的事情。但有一个特别的谈话，我仍然记得20年之后的一年。

So the head of the department came and told us that we were the best of the best. And that was why we’ve been chosen to do AP HD program at one of the top universities in the country. Now, this was supposed to be encouraged. It was supposed to give us the confidence and inspire us to do great things. But when he said that we were the best of the best, that little voice inside my head said I’m not. So I was sure that the others probably were that good, but I actually hadn’t done that well as an undergraduate, mostly because I didn’t really apply myself that well, and I just did enough to get through. And part of the reason why I’d applied to do AP HD was to kind of make up for that poor performance as an undergraduate and kind of show what I was really capable of. So I started from day one kind of feeling like an imposter.
所以部门负责人来告诉我们，我们是最棒的。这就是为什么我们被选中在全国顶尖大学之一做AP高清项目的原因。现在，这应该得到鼓励。它应该给我们信心，激励我们做伟大的事情。但当他说我们是最好中的最好时，我脑海中的那个小声音说我不是。所以我确信其他人可能做得那么好，但实际上我作为一个本科生没有做得那么好，主要是因为我没有真正地把自己做得那么好，我只是做了足够的事情来通过。我申请做AP高清的部分原因是为了弥补我作为本科生的糟糕表现，并展示我真正的能力。所以我从第一天开始感觉自己像个冒名顶替者。

So I’ll come back to how to overcome these feelings later. But for now, let’s just look at this assumption that as PhD students, you’re supposed to be the best of the best.
所以我稍后会回到如何克服这些感觉。但是现在，让我们来看看这个假设，作为博士生，你应该是最好的。

Now, in some ways, it does make sense if we imagine the education system as like a pyramid with compulsory education at the bottom of the pyramid and then a Phd at the very top, everybody who goes through that system gets continually assessed and graded and basically ranked. So the people who do very well have the option of moving on to the next level. Everybody else leaves, does something else. So by the time you get here, by the time you get to a Phd, you’ve probably done quite well at all those previous levels of the education system, but there is a problem with viewing a Phd this way and seeing it as the kind of ultimate culmination of the education system.
现在，在某些方面，如果我们将教育系统想象成一个金字塔，在金字塔的底部是义务教育，然后在金字塔的顶部是博士，那么每个通过这个系统的人都会不断地接受评估和评分，并基本上排名，这确实是感知的。因此，做得非常好的人可以选择进入下一个层次。其他人都离开了，做了其他事情。所以当你到达这里，当你获得博士时，你可能已经在教育系统的所有先前级别上做得很好，但是以这种方式看待博士并将其视为教育系统的最终顶点存在问题。

Because if we look at most of the education system, most of that pyramid, there is a certain consistency in the way that it’s structured, where you have a standardized syllabus to follow that’s been structured to help you learn. You have a clear timetable, so you know exactly where you need to be, what you need to do, and when. You don’t really need to think about it. And then you have a standardized exam where everybody does the same test based on the syllabus. So you have these clear boundaries between what you’re supposed to know and what you don’t need to.
因为如果我们看一下教育系统的大部分，这个金字塔的大部分，它的结构方式有一定的一致性，你有一个标准化的教学大纲要遵循，它的结构是为了帮助你学习。你有一个明确的时间表，所以你确切地知道你需要去哪里，你需要做什么，以及什么时候。你真的不需要考虑它。然后你有一个标准化考试，每个人都根据教学大纲做相同的测试。因此，在你应该知道的和不需要知道的之间，你有这些清晰的界限。

You also have another difference people often don’t think about is you have local competition where you’re graded or ranked relative to your peers, to the other people in the room. But in a Phd, there is no syllabus. So you have to design your own project and decide what to read, for example. There is also no set timetable, so you have to manage your own time and manage your priority. There is no standardized exam, so you have to defend your own personal work in front of an expert panel, and you have no idea what they might ask. And you also have global competition where you have to somehow make a novel contribution, which is, contributing something relative to the literature that’s already been published by the researchers around the world.
你还有一个人们通常不会想到的区别，那就是你有当地的竞争，你的评分或排名相对于你的同龄人，房间里的其他人。但是在博士中，没有教学大纲。所以你必须设计自己的项目并决定阅读什么，例如。也没有固定的时间表，所以你必须管理自己的时间和管理你的优先事项。没有标准化的考试，所以你必须在专家小组面前为自己的个人工作辩护，你不知道他们会问什么。而且你还面临着全球竞争，你必须以某种方式做出新颖的贡献，也就是说，贡献一些与世界各地研究人员已经发表的文献相关的东西。

So a Phd is not just different to everything that you’ve done before. In many respects, it’s the complete opposite. And that means that the skills that got you here, the skills that got you into AP HD, are not the same skills that will help you to complete one. And for many Phd students who’ve done exceptionally well throughout all those previous levels of the education system, the Phd might just be the first time that they’ve struggled academically. And if you’ve consistently been told from a very young age by teachers or parents or others, implicitly or explicitly that your value is in some way tied to your academic ability or achievement, then suddenly struggling academically can feel like a threat to your very sense of self worth.
因此，博士不仅与你以前所做的一切不同。在许多方面，情况完全相反。这意味着让你来到这里的技能，让你进入AP高清的技能，并不是帮助你完成某个技能的技能。对于许多在以前的教育体系中表现出色的博士生来说，博士可能只是他们第一次在学业上挣扎。而且，如果你从小就被老师、父母或其他人明确或暗示地告知，你的价值观在某种程度上与你的学术能力或成就有关，那么突然学术上的挣扎可能会感觉对你的自我价值感知构成了威胁。

And imposter syndrome might not just be the belief that you aren’t good enough to do a Phd, but the belief that you aren’t good enough. So let’s rethink this. So instead of seeing a Phd as the culmination or the ultimate achievement in the education system, I think it’s better to think of it as the bottom level, the entry level of the professional academic system. And what this means is that you’re not the best of the best. You’re a beginner, and you’re not expected to know everything. And, there’s gonna be huge gaps in your knowledge inevitably. And even when you finish your Phd, you’re still a beginner relative to somebody who’s been working in the field for years or decades.
冒名顶替综合症可能不仅仅是认为你不够好去做博士，而是认为你不够好。所以让我们重新考虑一下。因此，与其将博士学位视为教育系统的顶点或最终成就，我认为最好将其视为专业学术系统的底层，入门级。这意味着你不是最好的。你是一个初学者，你没有被期望知道所有的事情。而且，不可避免地会出现巨大的知识差距。即使你完成了博士学业，相对于那些在这个领域工作了几年或几十年的人来说，你仍然是一个初学者。

So the goal of a Phd is not to prove how good you are, but to learn and to develop the skills and the knowledge of a professional academic researcher. So then the question is, how do we go about this? So in the absence of a clear syllabus or road map to follow, how can you structure your own work and set your priorities to best develop your skills?
因此，博士的目标不是证明你有多好，而是学习和发展专业学术研究人员的技能和知识。那么问题就来了，我们该怎么做？因此，在缺乏明确的教学大纲或路线图的情况下，你如何构建自己的工作并设定优先事项以最好地发展你的技能？

So the first, most important, most fundamental point is to embrace what you don’t know. So as academics, the whole point is to kind of dance on the edge of ignorance and to step into the unknown with curiosity. So if you’re open about what you don’t know, then nobody can find you out. And instead of comparing yourself to other people, you can learn from them. So talk to people and ask the stupid questions, not only about things that you need to know for your own projects, but also ask people about their work, because most people quite like it when you show an interest in what they do and also broaden your knowledge and teach you things that you never would have learned otherwise.
因此，第一个、最重要、最基本的一点是拥抱你不知道的东西。作为学者，重点是在无知的边缘跳舞，带着好奇心进入未知。所以，如果你对自己不知道的事情持开放态度，那么就没有人能找到你。与其将自己与他人比较，你可以向他们学习。因此，与人交谈并问一些愚蠢的问题，不仅是关于你自己的项目需要知道的事情，还要询问人们关于他们的工作。因为大多数人都很喜欢当你对他们所做的事情表现出兴趣，同时也拓宽了你的知识面，教给了你一些你原本不会学到的东西。

So just a quick note here, since you’re at this conference and many of you have come from other departments or other places, whenever you go to these kind of conferences or summer schools, the talks aren’t actually in most cases, the most important thing. Because in all honestly, most of you probably won’t remember most, most of what you hear. The real benefit a lot of the time lies in meeting and talking to other researchers and making connections. But what often happens is people will stick with their own little groups or maybe they’ll meet 1 or two people and just talk to those people. So as a challenge to you, whenever you’re at these events, try and introduce yourself to, let’s say, at least three new people and connect with them, exchange emails, connect on LinkedIn, because networking is actually one of the most important skills that you can have for developing an academic career.
所以在这里快速说明一下，因为你们参加了这个会议，而且你们中的许多人来自其他部门或其他地方，所以无论何时参加这些会议或暑期学校，演讲在大多数情况下都不是最重要的事情。因为说实话，你们中的大多数人可能不记得你们听到的大部分内容。很多时候，真正的好处在于与其他研究人员会面、交谈并建立联系。但经常发生的是人们会坚持自己的小团体，或者他们会遇到一两个人，只是和这些人交谈。因此，作为对你的挑战，无论何时参加这些活动，尝试向至少三个新人介绍自己，并与他们联系，交换电子邮件在LinkedIn上联系，因为网络实际上是你发展学术事业的最重要技能之一。

So even in one of the coffee breaks, you see someone who’s on their own. Just go up and talk to them, introduce yourself. Because everyone will be kind of waiting for somebody to sort of open that, open that door. You know, they’ll be pretending to be on their phones, but actually it’s like, please, someone come and talk to me. So most people like it, so introduce yourself. So I’ll challenge you over this week, three new people a day. And then you’ve got, if you get into that habit, it’s gonna serve you well, in your, in your careers, but to come back to the point about learning from others and asking questions, at this point, the voice of imposter syndrome might start whispering in the background and worrying about how people might judge you if you ask the wrong question. But if somebody reacts negatively, if they’re rude or judgmental or dismissive, it’s not about you, they’re revealing something about themselves, most likely stemming from their own insecurity, in which case you just move on and talk to somebody else.
所以即使在喝咖啡的休息时间里，你也会看到有人独自一人。走上去和他们谈谈，介绍一下自己。因为每个人都会等待有人打开那扇门。你知道，他们会假装在手机上，但实际上就像有人来和我说话一样。所以大多数人都喜欢它，所以介绍一下自己。所以我本周将挑战你，每天三个新的人。如果你养成了这种习惯，它会对你的职业生涯有很大的帮助，但是回到向他人学习和提问的角度，在这一点上，冒名顶替综合症的声音可能会开始在背景中低语，并担心如果你问错了问题，人们会如何评价你。但是如果有人反应消极，如果他们粗鲁、评判或轻蔑，这与你无关，他们是在透露自己的一些事情，很可能源于他们自己的不安全感，在这种情况下，你就继续和别人交谈。

So the second point is that if you want to get good at what you do, it’s not enough to just set ambitious goals and work hard. So the way that you work and the way that you structure your goals makes all the difference in the world.
所以第二点是，如果你想在你所做的事情上变得优秀，仅仅设定雄心勃勃的目标并努力工作是不够的。因此，你的工作方式和构建目标的方式会在世界上产生巨大的差异。

So let’s say that your current level of skill is here, and you want to get to here. Now, if you set your immediate goals far in excess of your current level of skill, it doesn’t matter how hard you try, doesn’t matter how hard you work, you’ll just be frustrated. So what we need to do is have the confidence to set ambitious long term goals, but also have the patience and the humility to then work on gradually developing the skills required, recognizing that it takes a little bit of time. So if this gap is too big, what we want to do is simplify and set our immediate goals somewhere around here, somewhere at, or just a little bit beyond our current ability.
所以假设你目前的技能水平在这里，而你想要达到这里。现在，如果你设定的近期目标远远超过了你目前的技能水平，无论你多么努力尝试，无论你多么努力工作，你只会感到沮丧。所以我们需要做的是有信心设定雄心勃勃的长期目标，但也要有耐心和谦卑，然后逐步发展所需的技能，认识到这需要一点时间。因此，如果这个差距太大，我们想要做的就是简化并设定我们眼前的目标，在这里，在某个地方，或者只是稍微超出我们目前的能力。

And when we operate in this zone, because we’re pushing at the limit of what we can do, the work requires our full attention. And this means we need to narrow our focus and shut out other distractions. And also because we’re pushing at the limit of what we can do, it might take some time and it might take several attempts or experimentation with different approaches. And this means that we need to be willing to stay with it beyond any initial discomfort or frustration and keep trying. And although it might be slow and frustrating, this is where your skills develop. So as an academic, this is where you need to be kind of the most curious, the most engaged, the most enthusiastic, and most energized. 所以当我们遇到问题而不知道该怎么办时，可能需要一些时间。这可能需要多次尝试或尝试不同的方法。这意味着，我们必须愿意坚持下去，坚持解决问题，超越任何最初的不适或沮丧，并继续尝试。虽然这可能会缓慢而令人沮丧，但这不仅是你的技能发展的地方，也是发现发生的地方。因此，作为一名学者，这是你需要最好奇、最投入、最热情、最充满活力的地方。

So when you face a problem, try to slow down and focus all of your attention on it, perhaps by simplifying and breaking it down into smaller, easier problems and tackling them one at a time. Then even if you break it down into small problems again, it might take several attempts to get it right, but then it feels really good when you do. And this ultimately helps to reinforce your motivation and your confidence as you engage with the next problem.
所以当你面临问题时，试着放慢速度并集中你所有的注意力，也许可以通过简化和分解成更小、更容易的问题，并一次一个地解决它们。那么即使你再次把它分解成小问题，可能需要几次尝试才能把它弄好，但是当你这样做的时候，感觉真的很好。这最终有助于加强你在处理下一个问题时的动力和信心。

And speaking of confidence, and I’ll finish on this, and then we can get into the Q&A, so at undergraduate level or throughout most of the education system, the relationship between efforts and success, it’s quite clear, it’s quite predictable. So if you show up and you do what you’re asked to do and you learn what you’re told to learn, you can be quite confident in the outcome.
说到自信，我会说完，然后我们就可以进入问答环节了，所以在本科阶段或在大部分教育系统中，努力和成功之间的关系是非常明确的，也是非常可预测的。所以，如果你出现了，按照要求去做，并且学到了别人告诉你要学的东西，你就可以对结果非常有信心。

But in academic research, no matter how much skill or knowledge we develop, we can never really know what problems are going to arise, and we can never really know what the result will be. And even if you do everything right, or even if you approach everything the way that I’ve described, some things might go wrong in ways that you just can’t control. So confidence can not come from a place of certainty in the outcome. Also, no matter how much you learn, there will always be more that you don’t know and that journey will never end.
但在学术研究中，无论我们发展了多少技能或知识，我们永远无法真正知道会出现什么问题，也永远无法真正知道结果会是什么。即使你把每件事情都做对了，或者即使你按照我所描述的方式处理每件事情，有些事情也可能会以你无法控制的方式出错。因此，信心不能来自结果的确定性。同时，无论你学到了多少，总会有更多你不知道的东西，这段旅程永远不会结束。

So we can’t base confidence on the idea that we know enough. So true confidence, I think, comes instead from a belief in your ability to cope with whatever happens. So the belief that even if the Phd goes completely wrong, that you’ll be okay, you’ll figure it out. And it comes also from the belief that you don’t need a Phd to prove that you’re enough, because you already are. And if you approach it with that belief that an inner confidence, you’ll be all right.
所以我们不能把信心建立在我们足够了解的想法上。因此，我认为真正的自信来自于相信自己有能力应对所发生的一切。所以相信即使博士完全错了，你会没事的，你会找到答案的。它也来自于这样一种信念，即你不需要博士来证明你足够，因为你已经是博士了。如果你相信内心的自信，你会没事的。
